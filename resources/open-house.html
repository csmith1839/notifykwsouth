Office SOP · Lead generation

The open house playbook

An open house is two jobs at once: real service to a seller who was promised exposure, and the fastest lead generation a new agent can do. Both are won in the week before the sign goes in the yard. This page is the whole system — KW's official Open House Playbook, the MREA models, and our own market center training, in one place.

Why open houses — the MREA case

The Millionaire Real Estate Agent model is blunt about what builds a business: lead generation feeds a database, the database gets systematic touches, and touches become appointments. An open house is that whole engine compressed into one afternoon — NAR finds 53% of buyers use open houses to gather information, and most start touring before they've hired an agent. Be the agent they meet first. New people you meet go into an 8×8 (eight touches in eight weeks, so they actually remember you), then a 33 Touch yearly program — and roughly every 12 people you consistently touch produce about two deals a year. So the win condition isn't selling this house today. It's: the seller gets real exposure and a real report, every visitor enters your database, and your two or three warmest walk out with an appointment forming.

Myth-busting, straight from KW's playbook: "nobody buys at an open house" misses the point — almost everyone who walks through will transact eventually; the purpose is pipeline, not selling this listing today. Lookie-loo neighbors are future sellers, and visiting agents are future co-op deals. And weekends aren't sacred — a Tuesday 5–7 PM open catches the whole commute home.

Learn it before you host it

  1. Take the monthly open house training. We teach this system — the KW playbook, MREA, and lead capture — every month. Find the next session on the training calendar and in Market Center News.
  2. Shadow a seasoned agent's open house. Ask in the office Facebook group or grab a name from the team page — watching one run well teaches more than any checklist.
  3. Visit two open houses as a "buyer." Notice everything: how you're greeted (or ignored), whether they ask for your info, how it feels. You'll learn what to do — and exactly what not to.
  4. Read MREA's lead generation model (office has copies — ask the front desk), and drill your greeting at Conversation & Dialogue Practice. Scripts only get comfortable out loud.

Get the open house — and pick it right

  1. Claim one in the Facebook group — seasoned agents post their listings for hosting constantly — or use your own listing.
  2. Pick like the playbook picks — by Monday or Tuesday. The best draws: new-to-market homes (0–14 days), mid-priced for the area (widest buyer pool), low-inventory neighborhoods where buyers are hungry, and good curb appeal on a street people can actually find. Locking it in early is what makes the rest of the week possible.
  3. Choose the time for traffic, not tradition. Weekends: 11–1, 1–3, or 2–4 — or back-to-back slots. Weekdays: 5–7 PM catches commuters driving past the sign. Multiple opens on the same listing beat one.
  4. Set expectations with the listing agent (and through them, the seller). Agree on what you'll deliver: the marketing you'll run, the hours, and a report after — visitor count, feedback, and follow-ups made. That report is the seller value; promise it up front and you'll get invited back.
  5. Enter it in MIBOR by Tuesday or Wednesday. In connectBLC, the open house gets added to the listing itself (the listing agent does this if it's their listing — confirm who's doing it). Once it's in the BLC it syndicates to Zillow, Realtor.com, and the portals — that's where most walk-in traffic comes from, so early entry = more feet. Clicks fuzzy? The connectBLC knowledge base and MIBOR's free classes cover it.
The weekly rhythm (memorize this): Mon–Tue pick the house · Wed materials done · Thu marketing starts · weekend or a 5–7 weeknight host · same day thank-yous · Monday neighborhood follow-up calls and the seller report. Run it weekly and open houses stop being events — they become a pillar of your lead generation.

Materials — done by Wednesday

  1. Flyer. Property photos, key home and neighborhood details, your contact info, and a call to action stronger than "welcome" — the playbook's favorite: "Ask me about my price reduction list." Build it with Canva's KW templates, or let Command do it: Designs auto-creates six open house pieces for any listing in the market center the moment you search the address. Featuring a preferred lender on the flyer is common and allowed — but cost-sharing is RESPA-regulated, so ask leadership before splitting print costs.
  2. Sign-in — digital first, paper backup. Command generates an official Open House Sign-in Form — visitors sign in on your tablet or phone and flow straight into your Contacts as tagged leads. Add a QR code so visitors can register from their own phone. Print a paper sheet too (wifi dies; pens don't). Either way the form should ask consent to follow up — that's the TCPA line that makes your calls and texts legal, so it's not optional.
  3. Score cards. A short rating card — price, location, kitchen, yard, plus the two magic lines: "I would buy this home priced at $____" and "Are you working with an agent?" Visitors fill them while they walk; you get engagement, contact info, and the exact feedback your seller report needs.

Market it — starting Thursday

  1. Email your database Thursday; call or text Friday. Flyer plus one line on why this house matters. The next-day call or text — to people who've consented to hear from you — is what actually produces RSVPs. Start conversations, not just invitations.
  2. Door-knock the nearest 20 homes. Ten to each side, and the row across the street. The invitation that works: a neighbors-only sneak peek, 30 minutes before the public open. Neighbors love first access, they show up, and every one of them is a future listing. While you're at the door: "Who do you know who's been thinking about moving?" — and offer a free market analysis of their home.
  3. Post it — with video. Date, time, address, three best features, and a CTA ("sneak-peek video tour in comments") on your business pages. Post Thursday, repost Friday and the morning of. A small paid boost is optional — ads run from Command auto-tag responders and can trigger a SmartPlan automatically.
  4. Stage the signs. More than feels reasonable: front-yard sign mandatory, then directionals at every turn from the main road, major intersections, and each neighborhood entrance — eight is the floor, and KW's benchmark runs 20–40 in sign-friendly markets. Check the city or county sign ordinance first, place them the night before where allowed, and drive your own route the day of to replace any that walked off. Everything on-brand per the style guide.
  5. Know the house and the market cold. Pull the property's story with CRS Data and RealReports, comps from connectBLC, and neighborhood stats from Domus. When a visitor asks "what else is out there?" — that's your appointment forming. Be ready.
  6. Make a safety plan. Tell someone your location and hours, know the exits, keep your phone on you, and use FOREWARN on any visitor you'll meet with later. Meeting strangers is the job; walking in blind isn't.

Day of

  1. Arrive 45–60 minutes early. Recheck your sign route, lights on, blinds open, temperature right, sign-in station by the door. Walk the whole house and tidy what the sellers left out — and check the walk-up path too (find the dog's surprise before the first buyer does). Then shoot a quick "doors open at 1" video for your pages.
  2. Run the neighbor sneak peek for the early arrivals you invited. First look, easy conversation, and the question that matters: "How long have you been in the neighborhood — ever wonder what your place would bring in this market?"
  3. Greet at the door and sign in every single visitor. "Welcome! What brings you by today?" — then, naturally: "Sign in for me right here — the sellers like to know who's been through." No exceptions; the sign-in is the lead capture, and it's a safety record too. If they're working with an agent, ask who — you can let that agent know their client stopped by. Professional courtesy that gets remembered.
  4. Have conversations, not tours. Let them wander; ask real questions — how long have you been looking, what's the must-have, where are you now? You're listening for the two answers that create appointments: not working with an agent and needs to sell first.
  5. Capture notes between visitors. Thirty seconds in the Command App after each conversation (the 60-second log) beats an hour of trying to remember at 5 PM.

After — where the deal actually is

  1. Same day: thank-you text to every visitor who signed in. Short and human — "Great meeting you at 123 Main today. I'll send over the two other homes I mentioned." Same-day follow-up is so rare it's a differentiator by itself.
  2. Every visitor into Command, tagged, on a plan. The digital sign-in already added them — your job is the personal notes. Tag open-house plus the address (guide: add a contact right), then start an 8×8-style SmartPlan so the next eight weeks of touches run themselves.
  3. Book the warm ones. Anyone unrepresented and looking gets invited to a real buyer consultation this week — that's the Launch Plan's whole funnel in action.
  4. Monday: call the neighborhood back. The homes you knocked get a feedback call: "Great turnout Saturday — here's what buyers said about the area. Who do you know thinking about a move?" Strong open? That's your success story. Quiet one? It's a market-feedback story. Either version ends the same way: close for the appointment.
  5. Deliver the seller report inside 24 hours. Visitor count, score-card feedback (this is why you collected them), and the follow-up you're running. This is the promise kept — and the reason the next listing agent says yes to you.

When you're ready to go bigger

Everything above is the standard. These are the level-up plays from KW's top open-house operators — add them one at a time as the basics get comfortable. Agents who run the full weekly rhythm attribute as much as 15% of their entire business to open houses.

  1. Co-host the busy ones. Two or three agents at a hot listing means someone's always at the door while someone else talks with unrepresented buyers — nobody slips out uncaptured. It's also a great way for two new agents to split the work and the leads.
  2. Bring back-pocket inventory. Know 2–3 similar homes at or below this price point before you host. "Not quite right? I know two more like it — want to see them after?" turns one open house into an afternoon of showings.
  3. Go 100 doors, not 20. The high-level version of the neighbor invite: 25 homes up and down each side of the street and 50 across, knocked before the listing even hits the market — "Just Listed, and you're invited first."
  4. Capture the agents too. Other agents walk through constantly. Meet them, remember them — they're future co-op deals, referral partners, and the fastest market intel you'll get all week.
  5. Prospect on your buyer's behalf. When a committed buyer wants a street with no inventory, send letters or notes to homeowners there: "I'm working with a qualified buyer looking on this street." It's a listing-lead machine that starts from the buyer side.
The house rule: an open house without same-day follow-up is a Saturday donated to Zillow. The afternoon is the easy part — the database work after is the part that pays.
