Office SOP · Lead generation

The open house playbook

An open house is two jobs at once: real service to a seller who was promised exposure, and the fastest lead generation a new agent can do. Both are won before the sign ever goes in the yard — there's more work than showing up for the time slot, and this page is all of it.

Why open houses — the MREA case

The Millionaire Real Estate Agent model is blunt about what builds a business: lead generation feeds a database, the database gets systematic touches, and touches become appointments. An open house is that whole engine compressed into one afternoon — 10 to 30 face-to-face buyer conversations you didn't have to go find, plus neighbors who are all future sellers. MREA's database math says the follow-up is where the money lives: new people you meet go into an 8×8 (eight touches in eight weeks, so they actually remember you), then a 33 Touch yearly program — and roughly every 12 people you consistently touch produce about two deals a year. So the win condition isn't selling this house today. It's: the seller gets real exposure and a real report, every visitor enters your database, and your two or three warmest walk out with an appointment forming.

Learn it before you host it

  1. Take the monthly open house training. We teach this — open houses, MREA, and lead capture — every month. Find the next session on the training calendar and in Market Center News.
  2. Shadow a seasoned agent's open house. Ask in the office Facebook group or grab a name from the team page — watching one run well teaches more than any checklist.
  3. Visit two open houses as a "buyer." Notice everything: how you're greeted (or ignored), whether they ask for your info, how it feels. You'll learn what to do — and exactly what not to.
  4. Read MREA's lead generation model (office has copies — ask the front desk), and drill your greeting at Conversation & Dialogue Practice. Scripts only get comfortable out loud.

Get the open house

  1. Claim one in the Facebook group — seasoned agents post their listings for hosting constantly — or use your own listing.
  2. Set expectations with the listing agent (and through them, the seller). Agree on what you'll deliver: the marketing you'll run, the hours, and a report after — visitor count, feedback, and follow-ups made. That report is the seller value; promise it up front and you'll get invited back.

The week before

  1. Enter it in MIBOR by Tuesday or Wednesday. In connectBLC, the open house gets added to the listing itself (the listing agent does this if it's their listing — confirm who's doing it). Once it's in the BLC it syndicates to Zillow, Realtor.com, and the portals — that's where most of your walk-in traffic actually comes from, so early entry = more feet. Clicks fuzzy? The connectBLC knowledge base and MIBOR's free classes cover it.
  2. Build your sign-in system — digital first, paper backup. Command generates an official Open House Sign-in Form — visitors sign in on your tablet or phone and flow straight into your Contacts as leads. Print a paper sheet too (wifi dies; pens don't).
  3. Market it. A flyer and social posts from Canva's KW templates or Command Designs, posted to your business pages. Then the highest-value move nobody does: personally invite the ten nearest neighbors — knock or note. Neighbors show up, and neighbors are future listings.
  4. Know the house and the market cold. Pull the property's story with CRS Data and RealReports, comps from connectBLC, and neighborhood stats from Domus. When a visitor asks "what else is out there?" — that's your appointment forming. Be ready.
  5. Check your signs and your look. Directional signs (enough to be found from the main road), name rider, and everything on-brand per the style guide.
  6. Make a safety plan. Tell someone your location and hours, know the exits, keep your phone on you, and use FOREWARN on any visitor you'll meet with later. Meeting strangers is the job; walking in blind isn't.

Day of

  1. Arrive 45 minutes early. Signs out, lights on, blinds open, temperature right, sign-in station by the door, your materials staged. Walk the house once so nothing surprises you.
  2. Greet at the door and sign in every single visitor. Friendly and direct: "Welcome! Sign in for me right here — the sellers like to know who's been through." Digital form first, paper as fallback. No exceptions; the sign-in is the lead capture, and it's a safety record too.
  3. Have conversations, not tours. Let them wander; ask real questions — how long have you been looking, what's the must-have, where are you now? You're listening for the two answers that create appointments: not working with an agent and needs to sell first.
  4. Capture notes between visitors. Thirty seconds in the Command App after each conversation (the 60-second log) beats an hour of trying to remember at 5 PM.

After — where the deal actually is

  1. Same day: thank-you text to every visitor. Short and human — "Great meeting you at 123 Main today. I'll send over the two other homes I mentioned." Same-day follow-up is so rare it's a differentiator by itself.
  2. Every visitor into Command, tagged. Tag them open-house plus the address (guide: add a contact right), then start them on an 8×8-style SmartPlan so the next eight weeks of touches run themselves.
  3. Book the warm ones. Anyone unrepresented and looking gets invited to a real buyer consultation this week — that's the Launch Plan's whole funnel in action.
  4. Deliver the seller report. Send the listing agent (and seller) the numbers within 24 hours: visitors, standout feedback, and what follow-up you're doing. This is the promise kept — and the reason the next listing agent says yes to you.
The house rule: an open house without same-day follow-up is a Saturday donated to Zillow. The afternoon is the easy part — the database work after is the part that pays.
